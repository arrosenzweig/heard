1. Motivation

Heard began with the author's own experience. In her words:

"I am someone who has struggled a lot with keeping to my values and morals, even if they aren't seen as important to others. Especially in the MBA there is a lot of temptation to follow the majority opinion or cross boundaries that had previously been firm for myself."

She built it "to help myself and others have a resource to turn to when life is busy, your religious community is bleak, and when you need someone to listen without judging."

Who it is for

"Anyone who is struggling with their bad decisions, or decisions they have yet to make, or decisions that they have no power in."

Why "Heard"

"At the end of the day, people need to be heard for their true strifes and get real advice from a biblical rather than worldly perspective."

The motto, "I don't understand, but I want to understand. That's why I need you God," comes from one of her favorite gospel songs, "Why God" by Austin French (from his 2018 album Wide Open).

What is missing today

From her own search for this kind of help: "There is no AI that tailors the content based on niche beliefs and values. The only apps out there offer general evangelical or highly specialized content. There isn't an adaptable app for each person's own journey and values."

She draws the line at advice, not search: "The market might have AI scripture search, but it doesn't have the tailored and customizable advice. That advice comes inlaid with occasional scripture sharing and what that piece of scripture means." Section 2 tests this against two well-known apps and a plain chatbot.

From the start she asked for Bible verses, plain-language advice, perspectives from different denominations, and a way to connect with other Christians for community or to talk when needed.

Quotations are the author's own words, lightly edited for spelling.

2. What is new

The claim here is narrow. We compared Heard with two well-known faith apps and with a plain chatbot prompt; we did not survey the market more widely.

ProductWhat it is built around (from public descriptions)
HallowCatholic prayer and meditation: guided audio prayer, daily readings, Rosary and Lectio Divina, community challenges. Subscription ($9.99/month or $69.99/year in the US).
YouVersion Bible AppFree Bible app: many translations, audio Bibles, reading plans, studying with friends. A 2026 roundup reports an AI "life-situation" scripture search; we could not confirm this from a second source.
Plain chatbot prompt (our baseline)A single prompt asking for Christian advice with verses. It has no stored profile of the user's tradition, no verification step, and no way to connect the user with other people.

What Heard does that we did not find in the public descriptions of these two apps:

  • Tradition-conditioned counsel. Users pick one or more of 14 traditions (including "exploring") and may state convictions on 14 contested questions. Counsel speaks from inside that frame and never argues them out of it.
  • An approval loop on beliefs. A user can answer any contested question in their own words, by voice or text. Heard restates it plainly and uses it only after the user confirms the restatement is what they meant.
  • Verified citations from the user's own canon. Search runs over the complete text of three public-domain translations (97,993 verses). Catholic users get the Douay-Rheims, including Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach and Baruch. Every quoted verse is checked against the text before display.
  • Sponsor-style matching. Heard ranks people who came through the same struggle above people still in it, then by shared tradition and city. Contact details unlock only after both people accept.
  • An accountability agent. For each struggle, Heard checks in on the user's chosen schedule with three quick questions and an optional note, names the trend (overcoming, holding, deeper), and after two "deeper" check-ins in a row points the user to a person and a church. Commitments made in conversation are followed up the same way.
  • Faith asked once. Tradition and convictions are gathered only at onboarding, then shape everything; the user can change them in Settings. Every text box accepts voice.

Models. All evaluation numbers in this report were measured on gpt-6-luna (course Portkey key). The app also supports Claude (claude-opus-5-5) through the Anthropic API when an ANTHROPIC_API_KEY is present; that path was written but not yet run, because no Anthropic key was available when these measurements were taken.

Why only two model calls. Search, verification and matching are deterministic. That keeps the safety-critical steps auditable and cheap, and it makes fabricated references impossible at the retrieval step: a lexical index can only return verses that exist.

5. Safety

Risks

  • Missing a crisis. Someone suicidal, being abused, or describing a child in danger gets a devotional instead of help.
  • False alarms. A parent who confesses yelling at their kids is told to call 911, and may stop being honest with the app.
  • Fabricated scripture. Telling someone God said something God did not say, in the moment they are most inclined to believe it.
  • Misreading someone's faith. Conditioning every response on a distorted version of what a user believes.
  • Predatory contact. A list of people who disclosed loneliness or addiction could be misused by someone looking for vulnerable people.

Defenses

  • Triage on every message, in conversation and in check-in notes, not only the first.
  • Crisis halt. Hand-written guidance and real US hotlines (988, Crisis Text Line, National DV Hotline, Childhelp, 911). No verse, no advice, no matches.
  • A narrow, calibrated floor. Kinds that concern the user's own safety always halt. Outward-facing kinds with no physical danger get counsel with the resources shown above it. If triage fails twice, the resources are shown anyway.
  • Citation verifier on every quoted verse; the app always displays the real text.
  • Belief approval loop; an unapproved restatement is never used.
  • Contact gate: phone numbers unlock only after both people accept; city-level location only; decline and block.

6. Impact

Why the matching mechanic matters. Heard's human-connection design borrows from sponsorship in 12-step recovery. In a prospective study, Tonigan and Rice found that having an AA sponsor predicted greater abstinence during early affiliation, after controlling for motivation and treatment (Psychology of Addictive Behaviors, 2010, 24(3):397-403, doi:10.1037/a0019013; 182 participants with complete data). These details come from summaries of the paper; we could not access the full text. One study is not proof, and we have not reviewed the wider research. We cite it as a reason the mechanic is worth testing, not as evidence that Heard works.

7. Limits and next steps

  • Claude path unmeasured. Re-run both evaluations with an Anthropic key before quoting Claude costs or accuracy.
  • Safety evaluation is small and written. 14 fixtures, all using explicit language. Indirect or coded language is not tested. Next: a larger set reviewed by someone with crisis-line training.
  • Crisis resources are US-only.
  • Check-ins are in-app only. There are no push notifications, texts or emails yet, so Heard can only ask when the user opens it. A real accountability loop needs reminders that reach the user.
  • Voice needs Chrome or Edge, and in Chrome the audio is sent to Google to be transcribed. A server-side fallback was tested on the course key; its transcription models are listed but not deployed, so it was not built.
  • Retrieval is lexical. It can surface verses that match words but not meaning (for example a verse about God not hearing prayer, for someone afraid God is not listening). Counsel chooses among candidates, but semantic retrieval is the obvious upgrade.
  • No moderation of user-to-user contact beyond accept, decline and block. A real launch needs reporting, review and identity checks.
  • Tradition notes are the model's general framing, not drawn from each church's documents, by design (the Bible is the only content source). Users of a given tradition should review them.
  • No real users yet. No pilot yet.
